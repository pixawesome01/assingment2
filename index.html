Australian sport, venues and crowds

How Stadiums Define Australian Sport

Explore the stadiums, events and audiences that shape Australia's sporting landscape.

Sport is woven into Australia’s national fabric—with millions filling grandstands every weekend across Australian Rules Football, cricket, rugby league, and soccer. However, this passion is not housed uniformly. Across the country, venue architecture tells a profound story of regional preferences, historical evolution, and multi-code compromises. By comparing stadium types, seating capacities, and crowd distributions across states and territories, we uncover how infrastructure both mirrors and shapes the modern Australian sporting landscape.

01 · The venues

What are Australia's stadiums like?

Compare stadium types, capacity and the sports hosted across different venues.

The dual powerhouses: oval and rectangular dominance

Oval and rectangular stadiums dominate Australia's sporting landscape. Ovals accommodate AFL and cricket, while rectangular grounds primarily host football and rugby.

Ovals account for 34 of the 68 listed venues and rectangular grounds for 26. Together they make up about 88% of the dataset; diamonds and convertible venues are much less common.

Versatility determines crowd size

Oval stadiums bring the widest variety of sports together, while rectangular venues serve fewer sports codes. Blank cells indicate unavailable attendance records or that the stadium is not compatible with the sport.

Oval venues appear across the widest range of sports in the matched attendance data.

The engineering of high-demand venues

Convertible stadiums lead in average capacity, reflecting the scale of these adaptable venues. Their flexible designs accommodate different sporting events and large crowds.

Convertible venues have a listed capacity, at about 50,750 seats. There are only two such venues in this dataset, so it should be noted that the average describes a small group.

Urban concentration and coastal anchors

Australia's stadiums cluster around capital cities and major population centres. These urban hubs bring together different venue designs, reflecting diverse sporting interests.

02 · Across the country

How does sport vary by state?

See how stadium types, capacity and facilities are distributed around Australia.

Victoria and NSW have the broadest mix

Australia's states reveal distinct sporting identities. Victoria showcases one of the broadest venue mixes, Tasmania includes a convertible stadium, and the ACT has the highest proportion of diamonds.

Victoria and New South Wales have the broadest mix in the dataset; Victoria also has a diamond and an athletics-track ground. Tasmania’s listed venues are ovals and one convertible ground; the ACT has the highest share of diamonds (1 of 4).

New South Wales leads the infrastructure count

New South Wales leads with 20 major stadiums, followed by Queensland and Victoria. Sporting infrastructure is concentrated unevenly across Australia's states and territories.

New South Wales has the most listed major stadiums (20), followed by Queensland (16) and Victoria (13). These are counts from this dataset, rather than a measure of venue size or population-adjusted provision.

Small populations, outsized grandstands

Size isn't everything. Despite its smaller population, Tasmania leads Australia with approximately 101 stadium seats per 1,000 residents, outperforming larger states on this measure.

Tasmania has the highest listed capacity per resident, at about 101 seats per 1,000 people. Its smaller population raises this per-capita measure; this however does not mean Tasmania has the largest total stadium capacity.

03 · The crowds

How many people turn up?

Linear demand across Australian arenas

Bigger stadiums generally attract bigger crowds, revealing a strong positive relationship between capacity and attendance. However, crowd sizes still vary considerably across sports.

Across the 79 matched stadium-and-sport records, higher-capacity venues generally also record higher average attendance. Bubble size shows event count, and attendance still varies by sport and venue.

Western Australia’s crowd efficiency

Western Australia stands out for stadium utilisation. At Optus Stadium, football and rugby union attract crowds exceeding 80% of capacity, while rugby league records a smaller share.

Each state is represented by its highest-capacity stadium, so this is not a state-wide venue average.

AFL's story began in Victoria's historic suburban grounds before expanding across Australia. Over time, new interstate venues emerged while several traditional grounds disappeared from recorded AFL use.

Closing takeaway

Stadiums reflect how Australia plays

From historic suburban ovals to modern sporting arenas, Australia's stadiums tell a story of a nation united by sport, yet shaped by distinct regional identities.

Oval and rectangular grounds reflect the diversity of Australia's sporting passions, while differences in capacity, location and attendance reveal how communities experience sport across the country. As venues evolve and sporting crowds grow, one thing remains clear: stadiums are more than places to watch a game. They are where Australian sporting culture, community and history come together.
